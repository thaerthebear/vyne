Read lessonDo worksheet

Plan 10–15 minutes for a first draft; outside research can take longer.

Written lesson · 195 words

The ideaA rental rate is not your profit. First separate the costs that continue each month from costs that change with rentals.

How to do itSeparate one-time setup spending, monthly fixed costs and costs that change with rentals. Write the amount, source/date and whether it is an estimate. Choose one unit: a paid rental day. Convert a per-trip expense before mixing units: a $30 cleaning cost spread over a three-day trip is $10 per paid day in that example. Keep this planning calculation separate from tax accounting.

Worked exampleFictional math: $50 price − $20 variable cost = $30 contribution per paid day. Contribution is what remains toward fixed costs. $600 monthly fixed costs ÷ $30 = 20 paid days to cover those costs. At 15 paid days: 15 × $30 − $600 = −$150. If contribution is zero or negative, there is no positive break-even target using this formula. If the required paid days exceed available days, revise the plan.
